---
title: "About Deployments"
---

A deployment is something that you keep running: a site, an app, a service, or a place. Work does not stop when you deliver it. A deployment collects the work that comes up while it runs.

A deployment is abstract. It has no URL and no chain of versions. It is a name for a thing that runs, with an inbox for its work.

## What a deployment holds

- **Name.** What people call it.
- **Version.** Optional. A free label, for example `1.4` or `Spring launch`. Ops does not check it.
- **Status.** Active, Paused, or Retired.
- **Project.** Optional. The project that the deployment belongs to.
- **Notes.** Text that the page shows.
- **Context.** Hidden text for search and suggestions. The page does not show it.
- **Check-up.** Optional. A schedule of check-ups: daily, weekly, or monthly.

A workspace can have many deployments. A project can have many deployments too.

## Status

**Active** means the deployment runs now. When a deployment becomes active, the service makes its inbox section.

**Paused** means it does not run for now. **Retired** means it is finished.

The deployments list shows active deployments first, then paused ones, then retired ones.

## The inbox

The inbox is a section of the deployment. It holds the tasks that come up while the deployment runs. See [About Sections](/operations/concepts/about-sections).

A task in the inbox can have a tag. The tag tells you what kind of work it is:

| Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Feedback | Something a person said about it |
| Hotfix | A bug to fix now |
| Ongoing | Upkeep that goes on while it runs |
| Recurring | Comes back on a schedule |

You can add a task to the inbox, do it there, or move it to another section.

## Check-ups

A deployment can have a check-up schedule: daily, weekly, or monthly. At each due time, the service adds a task "Check" and the name of the deployment to the inbox. Then it sets the next due time.

Use a check-up to look at the deployment on a regular basis. Find bugs, add hotfix tasks, and move work to where it belongs.

## Recurring tasks

A task can repeat: daily, weekly, or monthly. When you mark a recurring task done, the service makes its next copy. The work comes back without a new capture.

## The loop

A deployment and its deliverables form a loop:

1. A deliverable ships to a deployment.
2. The deployment runs. Tasks come up in its inbox.
3. You select inbox tasks and start a deliverable from the inbox. The service makes a new deliverable with one section, and moves the selected tasks into that section. The deliverable records that it came from the deployment.
4. You do the work and mark the deliverable done. It ships to the deployment again.

Each turn of the loop is one deliverable. The deployment stays. See [Run a Deployment](/operations/guides/run-a-deployment).
